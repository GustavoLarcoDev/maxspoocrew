The software, and the model underneath it

Three questions, answered with the published rates and what the biggest operators in this niche actually do: what to switch on in Jobber, whether something fits this business better, and the changes to the model that move the most money. The short version: the software matters less than the billing frequency.

The verdict

If you only do three things

Ranked by money moved per hour of effort. The first one is free and is worth more than any software decision on this page.

HIGHEST LEVERAGE

Offer quarterly and annual prepay

A pet waste operator that grew to $3M reports customers who prepay quarterly are 72% less likely to cancel, and about 40% of their base chooses it. Churn is the single number the whole growth plan is most sensitive to.

Cost: a 10% discount · Effect: cash up front and a base that stops leaking

PRICING

Reprice twice-weekly

At $90 a month it earns $39 an hour including drive time — the worst of the four plans and half the industry benchmark. The second visit each week is nearly all driving.

Fix: $120/mo for parity with weekly, or stop promoting it

SOFTWARE

Find out which Jobber plan you are on

The growth plan needs on-my-way texts, quote follow-ups and route optimisation. On Jobber those sit above the entry tier. If you are on Core today, you cannot run the plan as written — and that makes this the moment to compare properly.

Decision: $0 to check, and it changes everything below

Jobber

What to switch on, in order

Configured in the order that pays back fastest. The tag on each says which plan tier it needs — that is the part most people find out too late.

“How did you hear about us?” on the request form — required

The one change that closes the measurement hole. The form is an iframe on Jobber’s domain, so Analytics cannot see the submit; without this field, spend and revenue can never be connected. Use the same options as the QR tags: door hanger, sign, flyer, trifold, menu, Facebook, neighbour, other.

ANY PLAN

Saved cards and automatic payments on every recurring job

Card on file at signup, charged automatically on the recurring schedule. It removes the monthly “do I still want this” moment, which is where a lot of churn actually happens — not in a decision, but in an unpaid invoice.

ANY PLAN

Client fields for the things that slow a visit down

Gate code, where to park, dog names, and a plain flag for a dog that should not be approached. Every one of those saves minutes on a fifteen-minute job, and the last one is a safety issue rather than an efficiency one.

ANY PLAN

Push notifications on the phone, and a stated response rule

Fifteen minutes, 7am to 6pm. A request is somebody holding their phone; the plan’s cheapest conversion lever is answering while they are still holding it.

ANY PLAN

On-my-way and job-complete texts

The texts are the product as much as the scooping — they are the only proof anyone came while the customer was at work. Put the referral ask in the job-complete message; that is a whole acquisition channel for one line of text.

CONNECT AND UP

Automated quote follow-ups

Somebody who asked for a quote and went quiet is the warmest lead there is, and today they get nothing. Two touches — 48 hours and a week — recover a meaningful share at no marginal cost.

CONNECT AND UP

A job form the tech completes at every visit

Gate re-latched, whole yard walked, anything odd photographed. It is the record that settles a dispute, and the photo is what makes the “we were here” text believable.

CONNECT AND UP

Route optimisation

Worth real money only once the route is dense enough to have an order worth optimising. Below about twenty stops a day, a sensibly hand-built route gets most of the benefit — so this is the one to delay, not rush.

GROW AND UP

The tier trap

Jobber’s entry plan is Core at $49/month, or $24 prepaid annually. It does not include two-way texting, automated quote follow-ups or job forms — those start at Connect ($139/month, or $80 prepaid) — and route optimisation starts at Grow ($199/month, or $120 prepaid). Four of the eight items above therefore cost money to unlock. Confirm what is active on the account before assuming any of it works: the industry page markets these features without saying which tier carries them, and third-party reviews quote older prices. The honest recommendation

If the account is already on Connect or above, stay on Jobber. The dog-specific features are nice, not decisive, and switching means rebuilding the website’s embedded form and migrating every client — real work for a modest gain.

If the account is on Core, this is the moment to compare. You are about to pay Jobber more anyway. Sweep&Go’s entry tier at $29 per staff member includes client notifications and route optimisation, which Jobber puts behind Connect and Grow. Run the 15-day trial in parallel before upgrading, and decide with both in front of you. The operator who took this business to $3M runs both — Sweep&Go for routing, Jobber as the CRM — which is a reasonable end state and not a contradiction.

Assumes 6 minutes between stops and on-site time rising with days of accumulation but not in proportion to them. The benchmark from a large operator in this niche is $70–80 an hour starting out, aiming well over $100.

Two things fall out of that table

Twice weekly is upside down. It is the most work and the least money per hour, because the second visit each week is a nearly-clean yard and a full drive. To earn what weekly earns it would need to be about $120 a month, not $90. Either move it, or stop selling it as the upgrade.

Drive time is the villain, so density is the fix — and it helps the frequent plans most. Halving the drive from six minutes to three lifts twice-weekly by 23% and the monthly plan by only 7%. That is the whole argument for door hangers over ads in one number: ads buy a customer anywhere, hangers buy the one next door.

The model

The best shape for this business

Dense residential recurring, sold on prepay, with a little commercial as ballast. Not a scooping service that also sells subscriptions — a subscription business that happens to scoop.

Bill quarterly and annually, at a 10% discount

The largest single lever available. Quarterly prepayers churn far less, and the cash arrives before the work does — which is how the print run and the ad budget get funded without a loan.

Quote per visit, bill per month

“About $18.50 a visit” lands better than “$80 a month” for the same money, and the trifold that does that arithmetic out loud already exists. The monthly number is what gets charged; the per-visit number is what gets said.

Refuse or surcharge the outliers

A yard more than about ten minutes off a cluster costs more in driving than it earns. Saying no to it, or pricing it properly, is worth more than winning it — and it is the discipline most new operators skip.

Two or three commercial accounts, and no more at first

An apartment community smooths cash flow and fills a weekday gap, and one is worth eleven houses in revenue. But they pay slower, negotiate harder and do not build route density. Ballast, not the main sail.

One-time cleanups are a lead source, not a line of business

$100 flat for a spring reset is a good hour’s work and a warm introduction. Treat it as the top of the funnel — every one should end with an offer to put it on a schedule — not as revenue to chase.

Do not hire until the route is full

Operators in this niche report a single technician handling 125–150 recurring accounts at 25–35 stops a day on a dense route. The $50K target is a third of that. A second person before then converts a profitable business into a busy one.

What this means for the $50K plan

The growth map assumes 4% monthly churn and 51 customers. Prepay is the lever that moves both. Lower churn means fewer gross additions needed for the same net, which pulls the target forward by months without spending another dollar on advertising — and the map’s calculator will show you exactly how many, if you drag the churn slider down.
