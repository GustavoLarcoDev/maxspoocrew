Why it matters

It is not just an eyesore.

A yard that does not get cleared is a place your dog keeps reinfecting itself, and a place your grass keeps dying. Neither one fixes itself with time.

Parasites
keep cycling

Roundworm and hookworm eggs pass out in waste and go back in through the paws and the mouth. Clearing the yard breaks the loop that a dewormer alone does not.

Giardia
lives in it

It survives in damp ground for weeks and moves easily between dogs sharing a yard. Households with more than one dog pass it around the longest.

The grass
burns out

Waste is high in nitrogen and salts. Left in place it scorches the turf, then the bare patch turns to mud, then the mud comes indoors.

It reaches
the storm drain

Rain moves what is on the lawn into the gutter and then into the creek. It is not fertiliser on the way — it is bacteria.

What actually helps

Removing it. On a schedule. All of it.

Picking up what you can see, when you happen to notice, is what most people already do — and it is why the problem persists. The volume that matters sits in the beds, along the fence line and under the deck, which is exactly the ground nobody walks on a Saturday.

We walk all of it, every visit, on the same day each week whether anyone reminds us or not. And it leaves in the truck rather than sitting in your bin until collection day.
