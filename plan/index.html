The map

Five stages, eleven steps, two loops

Read it top to bottom. Each card says what happens, who does it, and how long it should take. The amber notes are the places the plan leaks today — those are the whole point of drawing this.

STAGE 01

Attract

Somebody who has never heard of you sees something. This is the only stage that costs money up front.

STEP 1

A piece gets in front of them

A door hanger on the handle, a sign at the junction, a trifold at the vet, a Facebook ad, or a neighbour’s yard that is obviously clean.

Who: Mike, on a fixed weekly rhythm · When: continuous
STEP 2

They act on it

They scan the QR, type the domain, or call the number. Roadside signs almost always produce a call; print and ads almost always produce a visit.

Measured by: the utm_source on each QR · Target: 190 sessions a month

STAGE 02

Convert

From stranger to booked appointment. This is where almost all the loss happens, and where the cheapest wins are.

STEP 3

They land on the site

They read one thing: what it costs. The pricing table is the most-visited part of a page like this, which is why the rates live there and not on the print.

Target: 4 in 100 visitors reach the form
STEP 4

They fill in the form

Every button on the site goes to #quote, which is Jobber’s own work request form. Submitting drops a request straight into Jobber — no inbox in between.

Who: the customer · Lands in: Jobber requests
Leak. The form is an iframe on Jobber’s domain, so Analytics cannot see the submit. Today you can prove which piece drove a visit and not which piece drove a customer.
STEP 5

Somebody answers

A request is a person sitting with their phone in their hand. Reply while they are still there and they book; reply tomorrow and they have called someone else.

Who: Mike · Target: under 15 minutes, 7am–6pm
Leak. No response-time rule exists. This is the single cheapest conversion lever in the whole map — it costs nothing but a notification.

STAGE 03

Deliver

The free first cleanup. It is the offer, the sales pitch and the audition, and it is the only part of the funnel the competition cannot copy cheaply.

STEP 6

The free first cleanup

Scheduled in Jobber, done properly however bad the yard is. Cost: about twenty minutes of labour and the disposal — roughly $25 of the $79 it takes to win a customer.

Who: the crew · Condition: with any residential plan
STEP 7

They go on the schedule

Service agreement signed in Jobber, card on file, a day and a frequency chosen. Recurring revenue starts here and not one step earlier.

Target: 7 in 10 free cleanups become paying plans

STAGE 04

Keep

Churn is the silent competitor. Losing four customers a month while winning five is a plan that never arrives.

STEP 8

The weekly visit nobody notices

Same tech, same day, a text on the way and a text when it is clear. The texts are the product as much as the scooping — they are the only proof anyone came.

Target: keep monthly churn under 4%
STEP 9

Something goes wrong, and you fix it fast

A missed spot re-serviced free inside 48 hours costs one trip. The same complaint ignored costs $2,050, which is what a customer is worth over their life.

Who: Mike · Rule: re-service, do not argue

STAGE 05

Multiply

Where the economics stop being linear. A customer is worth more than their invoice if they bring the street with them.

STEP 10

The two doors either side

After every new yard, hang the neighbour door hanger on the houses left and right. The truck is already there and the yard is the proof.

Who: the crew, same visit · Cost: about 40¢
STEP 11

They tell someone

The referral is the cheapest customer in the business and the only one with no acquisition cost at all. It has to be asked for, in the “yard’s clear” text.

Target: 1 referred customer a month at full base
Leak. The referral offer exists on the website but nothing in the service asks for it. A line in the after-visit text is the entire fix.
The two loops

What makes it compound instead of just add up

A funnel that only runs forwards needs constant fuel. These two send customers back to the top of the map for free, and they are the difference between a plan that stalls at thirty and one that reaches fifty.

The street loop

New yard→Hangers next door→ Neighbour signs up→Denser route↶

Every yard added to a street the truck already visits costs almost nothing in drive time, so the same hour of work earns more. This is why a clustered route is worth more than a scattered one at the same customer count — and why the door hangers matter more than the ads.

The trust loop

Visit done right→Text with proof→ Review or referral→Next stranger trusts you↶

A stranger is handing over a gate code. Reviews and a neighbour’s word are the only things that answer that before the first call, which is why they are worth chasing as deliberately as any ad.

The plan, in four moves

Honest list

What is missing, worst first

Ranked by what it costs to fix against what it is costing now. The first three are free.

01

The new website is not live

It is sitting in an unmerged pull request. Every QR code on forty-one pieces, and every ad, sends people to the old site. An ad click that lands on the wrong page is money spent twice.

Fix: merge the PR. It is the only item here that blocks everything else.

02

You cannot see which piece produces customers

Analytics is installed and the QR codes are tagged, so traffic by source is measurable. But the request form is an iframe on Jobber’s domain and the submit happens out of sight. Spend and revenue can never be connected without closing that gap.

Fix: a required “how did you hear about us” on the Jobber form, with options that match the QR tags. Jobber then becomes the scoreboard.

03

Nobody owns the response time

A request that sits for a day is usually lost to whoever answered first, and it is lost after the money to generate it has already been spent.

Fix: Jobber notifications on the phone, and a stated rule — fifteen minutes, 7am to 6pm.

04

A request that does not book gets nothing

Somebody who asked for a quote and went quiet is the warmest lead there is, and there is no second touch of any kind.

Fix: two follow-ups — a text at 48 hours and one more a week later. Both can be templates.

05

No systematic ask for reviews

For a local service, the review count on the map listing does more work than any ad, and it is the one asset that compounds without spend.

Fix: ask after the third clean visit, not the first — by then they have something to say.

06

“No contracts” is still on the website

It has been removed from all forty-one printed pieces because Jobber has customers sign a service agreement. The site still says it in nine places, including Terms & Conditions.

Fix: the marketing copy is a find-and-replace; the Terms page is legal wording and Mike should read it before it changes.
