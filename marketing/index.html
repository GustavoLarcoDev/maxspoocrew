The QR on the back goes to the quote page

Every code on this page — on these cards and on all the pieces below — opens maxspoocrew.com/quote/, labelled Scan for your free quote. It used to open the homepage, which put a scan one click further from the only thing a scan is for.

Ours also carry a ?utm_source= tag so Analytics can tell a card scan from a hanger scan; the three cards above carry the bare address. Both land on the same page.

The bare code is in cards/QR-quote-page.png if you need it on an invoice or a yard sign. Full ordering instructions came with the files and are kept at cards/READ-ME-FIRST.txt.

Before anything goes to press

Four things worth knowing

Two facts were wrong on everything, including the website

It is “St. Louis & St. Charles Counties” — plural, because it is two counties — and the hours are Mon–Sun, because the crew works every day. Every piece here carries the corrected wording, and index.html was fixed too, including the structured data that had been telling Google the business was closed on Sundays.

Anything printed before August 2026 has the old wording on it. Worth checking before a reprint rather than after.

Running the neighbour drop

  • Only hang the street one where it is true. It opens by saying the crew was on that street. On a street with no customer that is a lie, and it is the sort that gets repeated to the neighbour it names.
  • No customer is named on any of them, on purpose. A name or a house number tells the block who pays for a scooping service, which is not ours to hand out — and the claim reads just as strongly without it.
  • Never put one in a mailbox. In the US a mailbox is USPS property and only stamped mail may go in it. On the knob, wedged in the door, or on the porch rail.
  • Hang them the same day the neighbour is serviced. The truck on the street and a visibly clean yard are doing more work than the sheet is.

The sizes belong to the printer, not to us

  • Business cards upload at 3.61 × 2.11 in, which is Vistaprint’s sheet for a 3.5 × 2 in card. Do not scale or nudge them on their canvas.
  • Door hangers are 4.5 × 11 in, which is Vistaprint’s size. Not 4.25 × 11 — that size is real, but it is GotPrint’s and Conquest’s. Each piece declares its own trim in its @page rule and the build reads it from there, so re-cutting the set for another printer is one line per piece.
  • Everything else carries 1/8 in of bleed a side, with live copy at least 1/4 in inside the trim.

The QR codes are tagged, and they are checked

Each format tags itself — ?utm_source=card, hanger, flyer, rack — so Analytics can say which piece brought a scan rather than just that print works. They are verified by decoding them back out of the finished PDF rather than by trusting the source art: a code that only fails once rasterised is exactly the kind that gets discovered on paper.

Changing any of it

Sources are in src/, one file per piece, sharing _base.css and a skeleton per format. Edit a price or a phone number there, then run:

python build.py — rebuilds the PDFs and the previews on this page.
python build_video.py — rebuilds the animations.

The build fails rather than quietly clipping when a piece overruns its sheet. That is how two of the door hangers above came to be shortened, instead of arriving from the printer with their feet cut off.
