The one number that changes every other decision

A customer on the weekly plan pays $80 a month. Businesses in this trade lose roughly 4–5% of customers a month, mostly to moves and pets dying — which means the average customer stays about 22 months. That is roughly $1,760 in lifetime revenue from one signup.

Against that, the best-documented operator in this industry acquires customers through Facebook at $124.25 each. So the honest question is not “can we afford ads”. It is how many can we buy before we run out of route capacity.

What this means in practice

If a month of ads costs $600 and brings four customers, that is $150 each — slightly worse than the industry benchmark, and still $7,040 of lifetime revenue for $600. The failure mode in this business is not overpaying for a customer. It is not advertising at all, or stopping after three weeks because the first month looked expensive.

The real ceiling is route density, not budget. A customer three doors from an existing stop costs you minutes; one across the county costs you half an hour of driving. Advertise the streets you already serve before advertising new ones — that is also the argument flyer L already makes.

Timing

Everything is decided in February

This trade is brutally seasonal. Operators report up to 80% of a year’s new customers arriving in the first quarter — the thaw exposes a winter of waste and the phone rings. It is August now, which is exactly the right time to be reading this and exactly the wrong time to expect a flood of signups.

The mistake is treating February as the month you start advertising. By February the auction is crowded, your pixel has no data, your page has no reviews and your ad account has no history to optimise against. The work between now and January is what makes February work.

Where the money goes

Four channels, in this order

Not five, not ten. The most common way small operators waste a year is starting everything at once and learning nothing from any of it.

  1. Google Business Profile and reviews — free, and first

    When somebody finally does search “dog waste removal near me”, the map pack is what they see. It costs nothing and it compounds. Ask for a review after the third visit, not the first — that is when people actually feel the difference. Every review is an asset the ads borrow credibility from.

  2. Meta ads — the volume channel

    Nobody wakes up wanting to search for this. Facebook creates the demand by putting a solved problem in front of people who had quietly accepted the unsolved one. It is where the customers are, at $124 apiece, and it is the ten ads below.

  3. Google Ads — the cheap channel, and the small one

    Cost per conversion runs roughly half of Meta’s — $63.60 against $124 in the same operator’s data — because those people are already looking. The catch is volume: only so many people search this each month in St. Louis. Take all of it, then go back to Meta for the rest.

  4. The van, the yard signs and the door hangers

    Not a separate channel so much as the thing that makes the other three cheaper. Somebody who has seen the truck on their street recognises the name in the feed. You already have the magnets, the signs and the hangers built.

The thing that beats all four

Across the research this was the single most repeated finding, and it is free: answer fast. Responding within five minutes makes a lead 21× more likely to qualify than responding in thirty. 78% of people buy from whoever answers first — not the cheapest, not the best reviewed. Meanwhile the average business takes 42 hours and 73% of leads are never contacted at all.

Concretely: turn on notifications for the Jobber form and for Facebook lead forms, and treat a new lead like a phone call, not an email. An ad budget spent on leads you answer tomorrow is money set on fire.

Ten ads, two of each type

Copy the text, attach the image, publish

Every ad below is complete: primary text, headline, description, button and the exact file to attach. The images are ones you already have in /print/. Facebook truncates primary text at about 125 characters on mobile, so the first line of each one is written to work alone.

Two formats are deliberately missing. Collection and Dynamic Product Ads both need a product catalogue, which a service business does not have. Anyone selling you those is selling you an ecommerce playbook.

Both videos end on a card that is not generated

The footage is AI. The last two and a half seconds — logo, phone number, website, offer — are ads/src/endcard.html, rendered at 720 × 1280 and composited on by ads/build_video.py. Generated lettering comes out misspelled and cannot be fixed without regenerating the whole clip, so nothing that has to be correct was left to the model. Change the phone number in that one file and both commercials rebuild in seconds, with no new footage.

Add captions with Meta’s own caption tool rather than burning them in, for the same reason. Most of the feed is watched with the sound off.

4 · Lead form

The form opens inside Facebook and pre-fills the name, email and phone it already knows. Converts at about 7.72% against under 3% for everything else, and comes in 20–30% cheaper per lead than sending people to a website. Expect roughly $30–50 a lead in home services.

These only work if you answer fast. A lead form fills the pipe quicker than the other formats, which is exactly why it punishes a slow reply hardest.

How to set it up

Targeting, budget and what to switch off

SettingUse thisWhy
LocationRadius around the streets you already serve. Start at 10–15 miles, not 50. A 50-mile radius spends your money on houses you cannot profitably reach. Widen only when the cost per lead stays flat for two weeks.
Location typePeople living in this location The default includes people passing through. You want residents with yards.
ExclusionsExclude ZIPs outside the service area, and exclude your 5-mile ad set from your 15-mile one. Stops two of your own ad sets bidding against each other for the same person.
AudienceDog owners, plus homeowners. Then a Lookalike of your existing customer list once you have 100+. The customer list lookalike outperforms interest targeting once it has enough rows. Upload it as soon as you can.
Budget$10–15/day now. $50–100/day February through April, as far as route capacity allows. Below about $10/day Meta cannot learn. Above your route capacity you are buying customers you will serve badly.
ScalingRaise budget about 20% every three or four days. Doubling overnight resets the learning phase and costs you a week.
When to kill an adAfter 5 straight days above your target cost per lead. Not after two. Daily numbers on a small budget are noise. Five days is signal.
Creative rotationSwap the image when frequency passes 4.0. Past that the same people are seeing it repeatedly and the cost per lead climbs.

The one thing to track that nobody tracks

Ask every single caller how they heard about you, and write it down. The QR codes on the printed material already tag themselves by format — flyer, rack, trifold — so scans are attributed automatically. Phone calls are not. Without that one question you will eventually double the budget on the channel that is not working.

Free, and it feeds the ads

What to post when you are not paying

Two or three posts a week, from the phone, taking five minutes. The point is not reach — an unboosted local page gets very little. The point is that somebody who checks your page after seeing an ad finds a business that is clearly alive.

Boost the winners, do not boost everything

When an organic post does unusually well by itself, put $20 behind it. That post has already proven it works on your actual neighbours, which is more evidence than any ad you write from scratch has on day one.
