The other market

The census already knows where the apartments are

Every place on this map reports what share of its housing is single-family detached. The remainder — apartments, condos, townhouses, duplexes — is the commercial market, and it is the same figure read backwards.

THE INVERSION

The worst residential ground is the best commercial ground

Clayton is 68.5% non-detached — the weakest place on this map to knock doors and the most concentrated to pitch property managers. Maryland Heights is 49.7%, Creve Coeur 49.4%. Switch the map to Commercial and two municipalities vanish completely: Ladue and Clarkson Valley have no non-detached housing at all.

One pitch can replace forty doors

WHERE THE VOLUME IS

St. Charles has the most units, Clayton the most density

St. Charles city holds about 12,500 non-detached units, O’Fallon 8,800, St. Peters 7,900, Chesterfield 7,800. But those sit at 25–41% of local housing, so they are spread out. Clayton packs 4,262 units into 68.5% of a small city — fewer units, far less driving between them.

Across the 61 places with data: ~123,000 units

WHAT THIS LAYER IS NOT

Housing units, not buildings

This counts units from the census, which includes a duplex on a side street as much as a 300-unit complex with a dog run. It tells you which municipalities are worth a morning with a property manager. It does not name the buildings.

The finding

Income does not tell you where the dogs are

This started as a request to find the wealthy areas. The data says wealth is the wrong filter, and three independent lines of evidence say it in different ways.

THE EVIDENCE

Housing type is the gate, income is not

A peer-reviewed study on a national probability panel tested what predicts dog ownership. Living in multifamily housing rather than a detached house carries an odds ratio of 0.20 — a fifth of the odds. Children in the home lift it. Income was not a significant predictor at all.

So: size by houses, colour by income — never multiply them

WHAT THAT LOOKS LIKE HERE

Rich places that cannot buy this

Clayton has some of the highest incomes in the county and only 31.5% detached housing. Twin Oaks has a $120,151 median income and about 100 houses in the whole village. Marlborough is 13% owner-occupied. Income sends you to apartment blocks.

Wasted leaflets: most of a wealthy street

AND THE OTHER TRAP

Rich places you cannot drive

The Defiance area has the highest income in this whole study and 18.8 acres between front doors. The Femme Osage corridor runs 31 acres per household — 56 times thinner than the county median. The money is real and a route there cannot pay.

Before treating North County as free money

North County is thinly served, and that is partly a judgement the incumbents have already made rather than an oversight. Coverage in this market tracks household income and lot size almost exactly. The northern municipalities have the detached housing — Bellefontaine Neighbors is 90.7% detached, Overland 86.4% — and median incomes from $44,000 to $78,000 against $107,000 to $250,000 in the west. An open field can mean an unworked one or a tested one, and this research cannot tell the two apart. What it can say is that Tidy Tails chose to base itself in Florissant, which is a small piece of evidence that somebody thinks it works.

The opening

What nobody in this market is doing

The geographic gaps are real but arguable. This one is not.

Nobody publishes commercial pricing

DoodyCalls, Scoop Soldiers, Yucko’s, Little Stinkers and Poop 2 Scoop all publish or will quote residential rates on the spot, and all of them make a property manager request a quote for commercial. Three vendors means three rounds of phone tag. A published commercial rate card costs nothing and is a real differentiator.

Only one operator is actually built for commercial

Yucko’s has been doing HOAs, condos, apartment complexes and municipal parks since 1990. The rest are residential routes with a commercial page bolted on.

The nearest real threat is not a scooper

Trash Butler and similar valet-trash companies bundle pet waste into porter service for multifamily, and property managers already buy from them. That is the channel a specialist should be taking, not another residential street.

Lawn care and junk removal are not competing at all

No St. Louis lawn, landscape or junk-hauling company could be found advertising pet waste pickup. That channel is empty here, in both directions.

Method

How the numbers were built, and where they stop

StepFigureWhere it comes from
Owner-occupied detached homes, three counties426,346 US Census ACS 2020-2024, table B25032. Directly counted, not modelled.
× households that own a dog181,623 AVMA 2025: 42.6% of US households. The conservative of the two industry surveys — APPA reports 16% more.
× those with a fence112,607 About 62%, from the Dog Aging Project. A volunteer cohort that is 83% single-family, so this reads high — treat it as a ceiling.
× those who would pay for this? No credible published figure exists. IBISWorld sizes the whole US industry at $270.7M across 2,198 businesses and publishes no penetration rate; APPA does not track the category; no franchisor discloses a customer count. The vendor reports that do circulate disagree by a factor of thirty. Any penetration number quoted online is invented, and this page does not use one.

Three limits worth carrying

The two percentages come from different denominators. Owner-occupied share is of occupied units; detached share is of all units including vacant. Multiplying them assumes detached homes sit empty at about the same rate as everything else — fine for ranking neighbourhoods, not fine for quoting a household count to the nearest ten.

Small places have wide margins. Huntleigh has 149 households and every figure carries roughly a third either way. Bridgeton, Black Jack, Twin Oaks and Sunset Hills all have income margins above $14,000. No recommendation here rests on any of them.

The dog rate is national. Missouri indexed above average in the last state-level release, but that was 2011 and predates a methodology change. There is no St. Louis figure. One rate is applied everywhere, which means it shifts every place equally and changes no ranking.
